Harfogram — English word meanings

When an English word is played in the game Harfogram, a short meaning of the word is shown. These meanings come from the file below, which is an adapted database built from two sources:

  • Open English WordNet, 2025 edition — licensed under CC BY 4.0.
  • Wiktionary (English edition), by the Wiktionary contributors, as extracted by kaikki.org / wiktextract (data of 2026-10-25) — licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0.

Because it contains Wiktionary text, the adapted file is published here under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 license. You may share and adapt it under the same terms. The rest of the application is a separate work and is not covered by this license.

Files

  • meanings_en.txt — one word per line, sorted: WORD<TAB>meaning1[<TAB>meaning2]. A meaning written as @BASE/pos means "inflected form of BASE"; the game shows BASE's meaning of that part of speech.

Changes made — 2026-10-30, updated 2026-11-10

  1. Only words of the game's English word list (the public-domain ENABLE list) were kept. For each word at most two meanings were selected: Open English WordNet first (the most frequent sense of the two most common parts of speech); for words not in WordNet, Wiktionary senses, preferring senses not marked obsolete, archaic, rare or dated.
  2. Proper nouns were not used. Wiktionary entries written only with a capital letter (e.g. Torah) were used for nouns and adjectives when no lowercase entry exists.
  3. Inflected forms ("plural of house", regular -s/-ed/-ing/-er/-est forms) were replaced by a reference to the base word.
  4. Definitions were shortened to about 110 characters (cut at a semicolon or a word boundary, marked with "..."), the first letter was lowercased, and a part-of-speech label (n., v., adj., adv.…) was added. Typographic characters were replaced by plain equivalents.
  5. About 160 very common function words and two-letter words that the sources lack or describe only as abbreviations were given short original definitions written by Gurcan Studio.
  6. Grammatical classification labels that some WordNet definitions of pronouns and quantifiers carry (e.g. "thing (object:), singular, negative pronoun") were removed from the definition text (2026-11-10).
